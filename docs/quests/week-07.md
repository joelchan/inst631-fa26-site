## 🃏 PLAY

**Hot takes: with data!**

1. 5 mins — Brainstorm six designed things you think are either **overrated** or **underrated**. Do it hot-take style, first, no justifications!
2. 10 mins — Pick 1 hot take and try to describe what it is about the thing that is underrated or overrated, as an explanation you can and should check with data (drawing on the overview from the read/watch on types of evaluation). You may have more than one explanation! This is play, so invent one if you have to :) E.g., "wired headphones are underrated because they make small talk easier at parties"
3. 15 mins — For each explanation, write out what kind of **data** (observations) you'd want to have to convince others you're right *and* your explanation is sound. Note! This step is *not* about method! It's about data: so, instead of "I'd run an experiment", try hard to name what you'd need to observe, like *"People who wear wired headphones smile more (and so do the people they're talking with) in the first few minutes of a conversation"*. Note that it could be something people do, or something they say (or something else entirely!). Try to brainstorm as many types of observations as you can! 
4. 20-30 mins — Brainstorm a sketch — yes, could be a drawing, or a table, or a few bullet points — of three studies you might run to get the evidence you need, at three different budgets, drawing on the overall methods space in the read and watch: 1) an afternoon, no money, 2) a week and five people, 3) a month and a real budget. Get creative! Your answers probably shouldn't just be bigger/smaller versions of the same study: think about the tradeoffs and types of data you might like to have, again drawing on the discussions in the read and watch.

Post your six hot takes (somehow mark the one you chose to spec a study for), and the three study sketches.

## ✍️ Reflection

Three things — a **Snag**, a **Compare**, and an **Ask**. There's no word count and no need to polish: bullet points and half-finished sentences are welcome, as long as I can tell which of the three I'm reading.

**Snag.** One specific place you got stuck, reread, or bounced off. Quote the sentence, or give the page number or timestamp. If you feel inspired, try to articulate what the snag is, but even the quote/sentence/timestamp alone is fine. *"I didn't really get ID 1"* is too vague to be a snag; better would be something like *"I couldn't understand Norman's distinction between real and perceived affordances (ID 1, p. 30)"*.

**Compare.** Reflect on your PLAY in relation to the READ or WATCH, and try to identify a *"huh. that's funny/interesting/weird/cool"* or *"ahhh i see/get it now"* reaction that connects one idea (e.g., a concept, a design heuristic, or something else) with something concrete in your PLAY (process or product).

**Ask.** One question you *cannot* answer from this week's materials and think is worth class time.
