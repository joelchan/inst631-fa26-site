## 🃏 PLAY

**The sparkle-button audit.**

Over the week, screenshot every AI feature you bump into in a product you already use — *especially* the ones you'd never call an "AI product", like the summarize button, or smart reply, or  ✨ that appeared in a menu last spring. Or something else!

Try to map your sightings to the names in Emily Campbell's [The Shape of AI](https://www.shapeof.ai/) pattern library. If you can't map the thing you saw to that library, say so (and make up a name!)

Then answer three questions about your collection:
1. Which ones do you **actually use**?
2. Which ones did you **turn off**, or wish you could?
3. Which ones did you **not notice** until you went looking?

Post the collection with whatever names you could put to it. 

!!! tip "Bring the collection to class"

    Post it to the shared board before Tuesday, and bring it with you — we're going to work on each other's in the session.


## 🔧 SETUP — do this before Week 13

We're building things together in class next week, so arrive with a working tool. Get access sorted, make one throwaway thing with it, confirm it actually works.

Browser-based options: [Websim](https://websim.com), [Claude Artifacts](https://claude.ai), [v0](https://v0.dev), [Bolt](https://bolt.new), Lovable. Claude Code or Codex are fine if you already live in a terminal — but whatever you pick has to give you a **link someone else can open**.

Worth knowing about [Websim](https://websim.com/blog/make-ai-apps): whoever you hand your app to can use it **without signing up for anything**, and its built-in AI abilities (language models, image generation, text-to-speech) are free to run with no API keys. That matters next week, when you have to get a real person to try your thing.

!!! warning "Whatever you use, don't put anything personal in it"

    These are hosted services and your app may well be public. Build the tool; don't feed it your data.


**If you can't get anything working, don't quietly give up — bring it to class Tuesday and we'll sort it out together.** We'll spend the last part of the session making sure everyone leaves with a link that opens, because next week is a build day and a dead free tier discovered at 6pm is a lost evening.

## ✍️ Reflection

Three short paragraphs — **250–500 words total**.

**Snag.** One specific place you got stuck, reread, or bounced off. Quote the sentence, or give the page number or timestamp. If you feel inspired, try to articulate what the snag is, but even the quote/sentence/timestamp alone is fine. *"I didn't really get ID 1"* is too vague to be a snag; better would be something like *"I couldn't understand Norman's distinction between real and perceived affordances (ID 1, p. 30)"*.

**Compare.** Reflect on your PLAY in relation to the READ or WATCH, and try to identify a *"huh. that's funny/interesting/weird/cool"* or *"ahhh i see/get it now"* reaction that connects one idea (e.g., a concept, a design heuristic, or something else) with something concrete in your PLAY (process or product).

**Ask.** One question you *cannot* answer from this week's materials and think is worth class time.

!!! note "On AI: Don't short-circuit (y)our learning!"

    AI wasn't there when you got stuck, it didn't make your play, and it doesn't know what you believed last week. A reflection that could have been written without doing the week does not contribute to your learning, and pollutes the class's learning space instead. Please be a good citizen of our learning commons!
