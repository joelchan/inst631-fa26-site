## 🃏 PLAY — a taster before class, the real run in class

**Turn on the screen reader.**

NOTE: this play is subject to change, pending Dr. Jordan's instructions :)

!!! tip "Keep the before-class part short: 20 minutes, then stop!"

    Your usability test is due at the start of class, so I don't want this to eat your week. Set a timer. This is just a taster: the real run happens in class. Twenty minutes of being confused is plenty, and being confused is the point: you'll bring that confusion to Dr. Jordan's demo, and the demo will make a lot more sense because of it.


Use the page you picked for the [Accessibility Testing Mission](../missions/accessibility-testing.md) (if you liked the page you drove keyboard-only in Week 10, you're welcome to use that one). This is the start of the mission's manual inspection (Part 1), so keep what you find! Which screen reader to use is in the mission page.

**Before class: set up and taste (~20 min, before you write your reflection)**

1. Set up (~5 min). Get your screen reader working, and find out how to turn it on and off, and how to make it stop talking (seriously, do this first). Check that you can hear it through your headphones.
2. Taste (~12 min). Open your page, stop looking at the screen, and just listen. Try moving to the next heading and the next link. Don't try to finish a task yet.
3. Jot down what confused you most (~3 min). That goes in your reflection.

**In class: the real run**

Dr. Jordan shows us how this is done. Then it's your turn, on the same page:

1. Pick the task: an ordinary thing a person comes to that page to do (e.g., buy the thing, book the appointment, submit the form).
2. Try to do the task, eyes off the screen (~35 min). Keep a running list of the times you:
	- heard something that told you nothing (e.g., "image", "button", "edit text", "click here")
	- couldn't find something you knew was there
	- couldn't tell where you were on the page
	- couldn't tell whether it was you or the page
3. Post your list to the Miro board (~10 min). It's ok if it's rough!

!!! note "This is not a disability simulation either!"

    Same as the keyboard in Week 10: you're using the screen reader as an **instrument** that exposes the page's structure. An hour with it doesn't tell you what it's like to rely on one. Daily users are fast, and have workarounds you don't. Keep your findings about the **page**.

## ✍️ Reflection

Three things: a **Snag**, a **Compare**, and an **Ask**. There's no word count and no need to polish: bullet points and half-finished sentences are welcome, as long as I can tell which of the three I'm reading.

**Snag.** One specific place you got stuck, reread, or bounced off. Quote the sentence, or give the page number or timestamp. If you feel inspired, try to articulate what the snag is, but even the quote/sentence/timestamp alone is fine. *"I didn't really get ID 1"* is too vague to be a snag; better would be something like *"I couldn't understand Norman's distinction between real and perceived affordances (ID 1, p. 30)"*.

**Compare.** Use your 20-minute screen reader taster from this week's PLAY. Reflect on it in relation to the READ or WATCH, and try to identify a *"huh. that's funny/interesting/weird/cool"* or *"ahhh i see/get it now"* reaction that connects one idea (e.g., a concept, a design heuristic, or something else) with something concrete in what you did (process or product).

**Ask.** One question you *cannot* answer from this week's materials and think is worth class time. This week, a question for Dr. Jordan is especially welcome!

!!! note "On AI: Don't short-circuit (y)our learning!"

    AI wasn't there when you got stuck, it didn't make your play, and it doesn't know what you believed last week. A reflection that could have been written without doing the week does not contribute to your learning, and pollutes the class's learning space instead. Please be a good citizen of our learning commons!
